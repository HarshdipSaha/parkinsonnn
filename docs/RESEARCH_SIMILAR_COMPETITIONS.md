# Research: what past competitions and the DaT-SPECT literature say about winning the DaT Parkinson's Challenge

- **Written:** 2026-09-06 (deadline 2026-09-16 23:59 UTC). Non-interactive web research; every claim carries a URL. Items I could not open (Kaggle write-up pages are JS-rendered and return only a title to a fetcher) are marked **[unverified]** and were reconstructed from secondary sources (GitHub READMEs, arXiv/PMC papers, DrivenData blogs).
- **Our situation (from the brief and `docs/RUN3_BRAINSTORM.md`):** public 0.339 log loss / AUROC 0.925 (rank 152); leader 0.219 / 0.971; top-10 0.219–0.244; #50 0.273. Under the binormal model in `RUN3_BRAINSTORM.md` §1.1, +0.01 AUROC ≈ −0.03 log loss in the 0.95–0.97 range, so **every lesson below is translated into AUROC first, log loss second**.
- **Budget:** ~46 GPU-h (Kaggle T4 ×2), 10 days, 3 real submissions per 7 days.

## 1. Executive summary — the 10 most transferable lessons

| # | Lesson | Evidence (competition / paper) | Expected effect for us | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Register to a template, then classify a thin striatal slab with a *tiny* 2D CNN, 5-seed majority/mean ensemble.** This exact recipe is what every ≥97 % multi-site DaT-SPECT result uses: MNI registration, 75th-percentile reference-region scaling, 12 mm slab, 72×72 px, 194 k-param ResNet, 5 seeds, nnU-Net augmentation, 30 min/model. | Budenkotte 2024 (n=1,740, 98.0 % / 94.1 % / 98.0 % on three test sets) [PMC10957699](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10957699/); Wenzel 2019 (0.972 acc) [PubMed 31473800](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31473800/) | AUROC 0.94 → ≥0.96 (≈ −0.03 to −0.06 log loss). This is the single biggest lever. | Medium: registration exists in run 3; add a 12 mm slab + small-ResNet arm (~3–5 GPU-h for 15 models). |
| 2 | **"Unrealistic" blur + noise augmentation** makes a DaT-SPECT CNN robust to between-site / between-camera resolution differences: 0.960/0.953 → 0.989/0.975 accuracy on two external sets (nnU-Net-style aug only 0.972/0.950). | Buddenkotte & Buchert, JNM 2024 [doi 10.2967/jnumed.124.267570](https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.267570) | +0.01–0.02 AUROC on a 10-hospital, three-intensity-regime test set (≈ −0.02 to −0.04 log loss). Directly targets our ultra-low-count 5 %. | Low: add random Gaussian blur (σ up to ~2–3 voxels at 2 mm) + Poisson/Gaussian noise to all arms; retrain. |
| 3 | **Localise → crop → classify with 2.5D (adjacent slices as channels) + ImageNet backbone** is the dominant pattern in every RSNA winner since 2019; pure 3D from scratch wins only with large n or as a segmentation stage. | RSNA 2019 [SeuTao](https://github.com/SeuTao/RSNA2019_Intracranial-Hemorrhage-Detection); RSNA 2020 [imaios review](https://www.imaios.com/en/resources/blog/pe-detection-ai-challenge); RSNA 2022 1st (described in [arXiv 2512.12887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12887)); RSNA 2023 [Nischaydnk](https://github.com/Nischaydnk/RSNA-2023-1st-place-solution); review of 31 studies: 12 favour 2.5D, 5 favour 3D [JIIM 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10278-025-01827-6) | Confirms the run-3 2.5D EfficientNet arm; expect it to be the best single arm, not the 3D one. | Already in run 3; give it more seeds and the blur/noise aug. |
| 4 | **Multi-seed × multi-fold ensembles are the standard; 5 members capture most of the NLL gain; stacking rarely beats a simple (rank/prob/logit) average and often hurts.** | 5 folds × 3 seeds (RSNA22 3rd, [darraghdog](https://github.com/darraghdog/RSNA22)); 18-model rank average (ISIC 2020, [arXiv 2010.05351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05351)); N=5 suffices (Ovadia 2019, cited in [arXiv 2303.16210](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16210)); stacking failed for Kelp 1st and BioMassters 3rd ([Kelp](https://drivendata.co/blog/kelp-wanted-winners), [BioMassters](https://drivendata.co/blog/biomass-winners)) | −0.005 to −0.015 log loss from 3→5 seeds per arm; keep the 2–3-parameter logit blend, do not stack. | Low: GPU time only. |
| 5 | **Do not trust the public LB; trust site-aware CV.** ISIC 2020: single-model public-LB correlation with CV "essentially 0"; the std of the public LB (0.0093) was 8× that of CV (0.0012); bigger ensembles gave a more stable LB. OSIC 2020: public LB (15 % of test) "didn't correlate with validation at all". DaT test size is undisclosed. | [arXiv 2010.05351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05351); [OSIC 1st](https://github.com/artkulak/osic-pulmonary-fibrosis-progression); [DrivenData thread](https://community.drivendata.org/t/test-set-size-and-final-submission-selection/11493) | Prevents over-fitting the 3/week quota to LB noise; each submission should be a *diverse* variant, because DrivenData counts the best private score automatically. | None. |
| 6 | **Site-signature features leak; remove them and validate leave-site-out.** OSIC winner: dropping the "Percent" features gave "a huge boost on private LB"; AMP-PD winner used leave-one-patient-out to remove seed dependence; TReNDS winner explicitly detected site-2 test scans and bias-corrected; Whale-do: group folds closed a 90 → 45 mAP CV/LB gap. | [OSIC](https://github.com/artkulak/osic-pulmonary-fibrosis-progression); [AMP-PD](https://h2o.ai/blog/2023/winners-insight-navigating-the-parkinsons-disease-prediction-challenge-with-ai/); [TReNDS 1st](https://github.com/DESimakov/TReNDS/); [Whale-do](https://drivendata.co/blog/belugas-winners) | Confirms run-3 decision to drop the four scalar site fingerprints and fit T on leave-pseudo-site-out OOF. | Done in run 3. |
| 7 | **TTA is not a free lunch on medical classification; only anatomically valid flips reliably help.** A 2026 study found standard TTA *hurts* on MedMNIST (−28.5 pts pathology) because of BN-statistics mismatch; intensity-only TTA is safer. DrivenData winners: "TTA mixed results" (Kelp), only h-flip helped (Whale-do). | [arXiv 2604.09697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09697); [Kelp](https://drivendata.co/blog/kelp-wanted-winners); [Whale-do](https://drivendata.co/blog/belugas-winners) | 0 to −0.005 log loss; validate on OOF before shipping. For DaT, L/R mirror is the one valid flip (Nazari 2022 mirrored so the worse hemisphere is always left). | Low. |
| 8 | **Log-loss specifics: temperature on held-out logits, clip the tails, average in logit/probability space (not rank, not power).** Rank averaging destroys calibration (fine for AUC, wrong for log loss); power/geometric averaging "does NOT work" for log loss; label smoothing lowers ECE but can *raise* NLL. Clipping at 0.01–0.05 is a standard Kaggle hedge. | [Laurae](https://medium.com/data-design/reaching-the-depths-of-power-geometric-ensembling-when-targeting-the-auc-metric-2f356ea3250e); [mlwave guide](https://evah.github.io/2017-02-05/ensemble/); [Müller 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02629); [fast.ai thread](https://forums.fast.ai/t/kaggle-evaluation-log-loss/235); [sklearn](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html) | Our combiner (weighted logits + T ≥ 1) is already the right shape; add a clip at [0.02, 0.98] as a fail-safe (cost ≈ 0 for a calibrated model; large savings on a catastrophic outlier). | Trivial. |
| 9 | **Uncertainty gating.** The Hamburg ensemble flags 4.3 % of scans as uncertain and catches 90 % of its own errors there; certain-case accuracy 99.8 %. For log loss, shrinking flagged cases toward the prior is a direct win. | [PMC10957699](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10957699/) | −0.005 to −0.02 log loss if our errors concentrate in the ensemble-disagreement tail (check on OOF). | Low: uses existing seed/arm disagreement; one extra scalar (shrink factor) fit on LOCO OOF. |
| 10 | **Shallow beats deep at n ≈ 1–3 k in neuroimaging; multiple preprocessings act as augmentation.** PAC 2019 (n=3,307 sMRI): lightweight 3D CNNs, "shallow networks proved more effective than deeper", multiple preprocessing pipelines used as data augmentation, MAE 2.90. BraTS 2021 (n=585): every architecture failed (top AUROC 0.62; 80 % of 420 models at chance) — a *signal* problem, not a model problem. | [PMC8374100](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8374100/); [PMC9562637](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9562637/) | Keep both frames (registered + CoM-native) as ensemble members; keep the 3D ResNet small. | Low. |

## 2. Per-competition sections

### 2.1 RSNA Intracranial Hemorrhage Detection 2019 (Kaggle, weighted multi-label log loss)
- **Data/metric:** ~750 k CT slices; weighted log loss. 1st place private LB **0.04383**; no shake-up ([SeuTao repo](https://github.com/SeuTao/RSNA2019_Intracranial-Hemorrhage-Detection), [RSNA news](https://www.rsna.org/news/2019/november-december/ai-challenge-winners)).
- **Winner (SeuTao):** three 2D pipelines — single slice × 3 windows, 3 adjacent slices × 1 window, 3 slices × 3 windows — with SE-ResNeXt101 (256 px), DenseNet169 (256), DenseNet121 (512); then a **sequence model over per-slice features** ("the key module"); TTA; multi-backbone ensemble ([repo](https://github.com/SeuTao/RSNA2019_Intracranial-Hemorrhage-Detection), [write-up pointer](https://www.kaggle.com/c/rsna-intracranial-hemorrhage-detection/discussion/117210)).
- **Transfer:** 2.5D channel stacking + ImageNet 2D backbone; context across slices via a second stage. For a single-label volume task, the "sequence stage" collapses to pooling over slabs — cheap to try (mean/attention pooling over 3–5 slabs instead of one slab).

### 2.2 RSNA STR Pulmonary Embolism 2020 (Kaggle, weighted log loss)
- **Winner (Guanshuo Xu):** SE-ResNeXt-50/101 at 640→576 px after lung localisation; 3-channel input = PE window of the slice and its two neighbours (2.5D); bi-GRU over slices; BCE; light aug (contrast, shift-scale-rotate, cutout); **no CV** on this large, clean dataset; image-level AUC 0.96 ([imaios](https://www.imaios.com/en/resources/blog/pe-detection-ai-challenge)). 2nd used ResNeSt-50 + transformers + a 3D ip-CSN-101 on heart crops; 3rd used 3-window RGB (lung/PE/mediastinal) with a 31-slice window; 2nd-place paper and code are public ([Radiology AI](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/full/10.1148/ryai.2021210068), [i-pan](https://github.com/i-pan/kaggle-rsna-pe)).
- **Transfer:** localise first, then classify at higher resolution; multi-window channels ≈ our multiple intensity normalisations (reference-region, percentile, log) as channels — an untested but cheap idea.

### 2.3 RSNA-MICCAI Brain Tumor Radiogenomic (MGMT) 2021 (Kaggle, AUROC) — *why it failed*
- **Data:** 585 training cases, mpMRI (T1, T1CE, T2, FLAIR) ([arXiv 2107.02314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02314)). 1st place AUROC **0.62**; on external validation the 1st-place solution scored AUROC **56.2 %** / accuracy 54.8 %; in a 420-model sweep (EfficientNet-B0, SEResNet50, SEResNeXt50, DenseNet121 × 2D/3D × 7 sequence combos) **80.2 % of models were indistinguishable from chance** ([PMC9562637](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9562637/)). Authors' conclusion: MGMT status is not visible on MRI. 1st place code is a 3D ResNet-10 ([FirasBaba/rsna-resnet10](https://github.com/FirasBaba/rsna-resnet10)); the winner's own "mostly luck" comment is **[unverified]** (Kaggle post not fetchable).
- **Transfer:** the failure was the label, not the model — our OOF AUROC 0.94 says signal exists; but it also warns that at n ≈ 600 with AUROC < 0.7, leaderboard position is noise. Our public-LB rank differences within ±0.01 log loss are probably noise too.

### 2.4 RSNA 2022 Cervical Spine Fracture (Kaggle, weighted log loss)
- **Winner (Qishen Ha):** stage 1 segmentation → per-vertebra crops; stage 2 **2.5D CNN–LSTM**: 15 slices sampled along z per vertebra, neighbours + segmentation mask as channels, EfficientNet-V2-S / ConvNeXt encoders, LSTM head; patient level = all 7 vertebrae (7×15 slices) through the same net; ensemble across backbones and folds ([description in arXiv 2512.12887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12887), [inference notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/haqishen/rsna-2022-1st-place-solution-inference)). 3rd (darraghdog): 4-model cascade, 96 DICOMs → 32 × 3-channel 2.5D images, **5-fold × 3 seeds**, EfficientNet-B1/ResNeSt50/SeResNeXt50, overlapping-window TTA ([repo](https://github.com/darraghdog/RSNA22)). Post-hoc evaluation of winners: [PubMed 38169426](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38169426/).
- **Transfer:** anatomical localisation is done *before* the classifier; our registration plays that role. Seeds × folds = 15 checkpoints is a normal budget.

### 2.5 RSNA 2023 Abdominal Trauma (Kaggle, weighted log loss)
- **Winner (Team Oxygen / Nischay):** 3D segmentation → organ crops → 96 equidistant slices reshaped to (32, 3, 384, 384) 2.5D → CoaT-Lite-Medium/Small and EfficientNet-V2-S + GRU; **auxiliary Dice loss on masks gave +0.01 to +0.03**; soft labels from organ visibility; 4-fold GroupKFold; best single CV 0.326, ensemble 0.31x; light aug only (perspective, flips, ±25° rotation); **"No major postprocessing"**; slice-level ensemble then max per patient ([repo](https://github.com/Nischaydnk/RSNA-2023-1st-place-solution)). Baseline weighted log loss ≈ 0.65 → 0.35; "barely 80 teams out of 1000s" beat the baseline ([tweet](https://x.com/nischay_twt/status/1715978385476788320)).
- **Transfer:** an auxiliary regression head on registered-ROI SBRs (putamen/caudate L/R) is the analogue of the Dice auxiliary loss — cheap, and known to help on small-n log-loss tasks.

### 2.6 RSNA 2024 Lumbar Spine (Kaggle, weighted log loss)
- 1,874 teams; winners "Avengers" and "SonySpine s & tkmn & Moyashii" (Educational Merit Awards) ([RSNA](https://www.rsna.org/media/press/i/2554)). 1st-place write-up not fetchable **[unverified]** ([link](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/writeups/avengers-1st-place-solution)). **2nd place (Artley/Pan/…):** stage-1 keypoint localisation on sagittal images (2D backbone + attention head) → crops → stage-2 encoder + LSTM + attention pooling over 5–24 frames; **9-rotation TTA**; 0.5·loss(labels) + 0.5·loss(pseudo-labels); confident-learning removal of noisy labels via **out-of-fold ensemble**; manifold mixup; heavy aug incl. L/R recombination; axial pipeline improved CV 0.01–0.02 but was dropped ([repo](https://github.com/brendanartley/RSNA-2024-Competition)).
- **Transfer:** OOF-disagreement label cleaning is a legitimate way to handle label noise from 10 hospitals' readers (flag, down-weight, don't delete blindly — Kelp 1st found removing "mislabelled" images did not help).

### 2.7 RSNA 2025 Intracranial Aneurysm (Kaggle, weighted AUC-type metric)
- 1,147 teams ([RSNA](https://www.rsna.org/news/2025/november/2025-ai-challenge-winners)). 1st place title "Location-Aware Aneurysm Detection via Vessel-ROI Masking" ([write-up, JS-only](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/1st-place-solution)) **[content unverified]**. **2nd:** fast 2D tri-axial ROI extraction → 3D multi-task nnU-Net (classification + aneurysm seg + vessel seg), dual decoders, **two-fold ensemble** ([arXiv 2606.26706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26706)). **7th (MIC-DKFZ):** nnU-Net ResEnc-M on a 200×160×160 mm ROI, 1,500 epochs, 4.5 days on 4×A100, optional TTA, Apache-2.0 ([repo](https://github.com/MIC-DKFZ/kaggle-rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection-2025-solution)).
- **Transfer:** ROI masking = "make the network look only where the signal is"; our registered striatal box does this; a vessel-mask analogue would be a striatal/reference-region mask channel.

### 2.8 OSIC Pulmonary Fibrosis 2020 (Kaggle, Laplace log-likelihood, n ≈ 176 patients)
- **Winner (Kulakov):** blend of EfficientNet-B5 on CT + tabular **quantile-regression MLP**, QR weighted higher; **removing "Percent" features "gave a huge boost on private LB"**; abandoned quantile selection, fixed quantile 0.5, inference 3 min; public LB = 15 % of test and "didn't correlate with the validation at all" ([repo](https://github.com/artkulak/osic-pulmonary-fibrosis-progression)).
- **Transfer:** with tiny n, simpler and better-regularised beats bigger; features that encode acquisition/site are liabilities on the private set.

### 2.9 TReNDS Neuroimaging 2020 (Kaggle, ~5.8 k subjects, sMRI/fMRI maps, two scanner sites)
- **Winner (Churkin & Simakov):** Incremental PCA (200 comps → 1,200 feats) and dictionary learning on fMRI spatial maps, statistics of FNC/loading tables; **ElasticNet site classifier "detected ~1,400 new site-2 observations"** in test and bias-corrected them; 7 validation seeds blended + post-processing; 47 h of DL feature extraction ([repo](https://github.com/DESimakov/TReNDS/), [GSU news](https://news.gsu.edu/2020/10/21/brain-imaging-contest-mental-illness-research/)). 3rd (shimacos37): 3D-CNN features + SVR/Ridge/LGBM/SVM/NuSVM/KNN with LightGBM+SVM stacking ([repo](https://github.com/shimacos37/kaggle-trends-3rd-place-solution)). 6th: 3D CNN autoencoders + PCA ([write-up](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/trends-assessment-prediction/writeups/ods-ai-5d-brain-6th-place-solution)).
- **Transfer:** on registered brain maps, linear models on compressed features are competitive with CNNs; explicit site detection + per-site correction was the winning edge. Our ROI-LR arm is this family; make sure it sees *registered* ROI SBRs and consider per-pseudo-site intensity standardisation.

### 2.10 SIIM-ISIC Melanoma 2020 (Kaggle, AUC, 33 k images, 1.76 % positives)
- **Winner (Ha, Liu, Liu):** 18 models, EfficientNet-B3…B7 / SE-ResNeXt101 / ResNeSt101, input 384–896 px, 9-class diagnosis target (take MEL prob), 4 of 18 use metadata, 5-fold on 2018+2019+2020 data; **final = simple average of probability ranks**; CV 0.9600 → private 0.9490 → public 0.9442; per-model std: cv_all 0.0012 < cv_2020 0.0043 < private 0.0060 < public 0.0093; "we did not use public LB feedback in any way"; heavy albumentations aug; 15 epochs, cosine with 1 warm-up epoch ([arXiv 2010.05351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05351), [repo](https://github.com/haqishen/SIIM-ISIC-Melanoma-Classification-1st-Place-Solution)). TTA count **[unverified]** (not in paper).
- **Transfer:** richer target than the binary label helps (we have none, but the ROI-SBR auxiliary plays that role); rank averaging is AUC-specific — for log loss re-calibrate after any rank step or avoid it.

### 2.11 Kaggle Parkinson's competitions (different modality)
- **AMP-PD Progression 2023 (SMAPE, tabular proteomics):** 1st = simple average of LightGBM (87-class formulation) and an MLP with SMAPE loss, features = visit month / horizon / visit-pattern indicators; blood data "no meaningful signals"; **leave-one-patient-out CV** to eliminate seed dependence ([H2O blog](https://h2o.ai/blog/2023/winners-insight-navigating-the-parkinsons-disease-prediction-challenge-with-ai/)).
- **Freezing of Gait 2023 (mAP, accelerometer):** 24,862 solutions; top-5 all GRU/LSTM or transformer, "almost all ensemble-based"; private mAP 0.514 / 0.451 / 0.436 / 0.417 / 0.390; key trick: train on short sequences, infer on long ones ([PMC11156937](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11156937/)); an 8th-place simple 1D-CNN scored 0.356 ([arXiv 2307.03475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03475)).
- **Transfer:** patient/site-grouped CV and averaging two different model families were the constants.

### 2.12 PAC 2019 brain-age (registered sMRI, n=3,307, two sites)
- Winner: "lightweight 3D CNNs (FCN/VGG-style) combined with different preprocessing steps to form a multimodal ensemble", MAE 2.90–2.95; **shallow > deep**; multiple preprocessing pipelines "functioned as data augmentation"; transfer from 14,503 UK Biobank scans helped; non-DL methods reached 3.09 ([PMC8374100](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8374100/)).
- **Transfer:** the clearest neuroimaging evidence that (registered input + small 3D CNN + preprocessing-diverse ensemble) is the right shape at our n.

## 4. Log-loss playbook (what winners actually did)

| Technique | Evidence | Number | Use for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seed/fold ensembling is the primary log-loss reducer | RSNA22 3rd: 5 folds × 3 seeds ([repo](https://github.com/darraghdog/RSNA22)); RSNA23 1st: single CV 0.326 → ensemble 0.31x ([repo](https://github.com/Nischaydnk/RSNA-2023-1st-place-solution)); Ovadia 2019: N=5 "produces good results" ([cited](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16210)) | ≈ −0.01 to −0.015 on weighted log loss from ensembling in RSNA23 | ≥5 seeds per arm before any other tuning |
| Average in probability/logit space; **rank averaging only for AUC** | ISIC winner rank-averaged for AUC ([arXiv 2010.05351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05351)); power/geometric averaging "does NOT work" for log loss ([Laurae](https://medium.com/data-design/reaching-the-depths-of-power-geometric-ensembling-when-targeting-the-auc-metric-2f356ea3250e)); rank averaging is for mis-calibrated members ([mlwave](https://evah.github.io/2017-02-05/ensemble/)) | — | keep weighted-logit blend |
| Temperature scaling on a held-out set; does not change accuracy | [sklearn calibration](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html) | 1 parameter | fit T on LOCO-pooled OOF (done); floor at 1.0 is conservative and correct for a public/private shift |
| Clip probabilities away from 0/1 | Kaggle practice, thresholds tested 0.001 / 0.01 / 0.05 ([fast.ai thread](https://forums.fast.ai/t/kaggle-evaluation-log-loss/235)) | one wrong 0.999 costs 6.9 nats; clipped at 0.98 costs 3.9 | clip to [0.02, 0.98]; on a well-calibrated model the expected cost is < 0.001 |
| Label smoothing: better ECE, **can worsen NLL** | Müller 2019 ([arXiv 1906.02629](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02629)) | ECE 0.150 → 0.024 at α=0.05 but NLL sometimes worse | do not add; use T instead (our λ=0.02 shrink to prior is equivalent and measured cost 0.001) |
| No post-processing beyond averaging | RSNA23 1st: "No major postprocessing" | — | resist hand rules |
| Auxiliary targets improve the main log loss | RSNA23 aux Dice +0.01–0.03; ISIC 9-class target | — | aux SBR regression head |
| Hedge uncertain cases toward the prior | Hamburg UDM: 4.3 % flagged, 90 % of errors caught ([PMC10957699](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10957699/)) | certain-case acc 99.8 % | shrink flagged cases: p' = (1−s)·p + s·π with s fit on OOF |
| Simpler is safer under tiny n | OSIC winner: QR weighted higher than the CNN ([repo](https://github.com/artkulak/osic-pulmonary-fibrosis-progression)) | — | keep ROI-LR in the blend with a non-zero weight if LOCO says so |

Reference points for "how far a good blend can go" under a log-loss metric at n ≈ 1–2 k: RSNA23 (n=3 k studies) improved the baseline 0.65 → 0.35 with a full two-stage pipeline; BraTS (n=585, no signal) stayed at AUROC 0.62. Our 0.925 → 0.97 AUROC target sits between "signal exists" and "expert-level"; the DaT literature says 0.97+ is reachable with registration + slab CNN on ≥1 k scans.

## 5. Multi-site / domain-shift playbook

| Practice | Evidence | Our mapping |
|---|---|---|
| Leave-site-out (or group) CV; random k-fold "can drastically overestimate results" | [PMC8675424](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8675424/), [Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroinformatics/articles/10.3389/fninf.2021.709179/full); Whale-do 90 → 45 mAP gap closed by group folds ([blog](https://drivendata.co/blog/belugas-winners)) | leave-pseudo-site-out folds for T and blend weights (run 3) |
| Detect the shifted site in test and correct | TReNDS 1st: ElasticNet site-2 detector, ~1,400 test scans bias-corrected ([repo](https://github.com/DESimakov/TReNDS/)) | pseudo-site clustering on header/grid/intensity is allowed at inference (per-scan, no pseudo-labelling): use it only for *normalisation*, never as a feature |
| Train on mixed resolutions; augment beyond realism | Wenzel: mixed acc 0.955 vs SBR 0.900 ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31473800/)); Buddenkotte 2024: blur+noise aug 0.960 → 0.989 ([doi](https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.267570)) | random Gaussian blur σ ∈ [0, 3] vox and Poisson/Gaussian noise at random count levels on every arm; simulate the ultra-low-count regime by scaling counts down and resampling |
| Reference-region intensity scaling, not global min-max | Hamburg 75th-percentile of brain-minus-striatum ([PMC8921148](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8921148/)) | run 3 does this; also feed a *second* normalisation (e.g. whole-brain p95) as an extra channel for diversity (PE-2020 multi-window analogue) |
| L/R canonicalisation | Nazari mirrored so the worse side is left | replaces flip-TTA with a deterministic mirror; or use flip as the only TTA |
| Drop site-fingerprint scalars | OSIC "Percent" features; our own LOCO measurement (97.3 % cluster ID accuracy) | done in run 3 |
| Multiple preprocessings as augmentation/ensemble diversity | PAC 2019 ([PMC8374100](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8374100/)) | keep CoM-native checkpoints in the blend with registered ones |
| Smaller-site/lower-quality data drops AUROC by ~5 pts even for SOTA | JIIM 2026 dataset A 92.6 % vs 97.4–98.6 % ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10278-025-01831-w)) | expect the low-count 5 % to dominate residual log loss; that is where uncertainty hedging pays |

## 6. Code-execution playbook (DrivenData)

**Platform facts.** Submissions run as containers on Azure Kubernetes with **no network**, read-only test data; zips are "typically no more than a few hundred MBs"; participants must test locally "using the exact same runtime" (Docker image from Docker Hub) because "code written for one runtime could very easily fail to run on another" ([DrivenData blog](https://drivendata.co/blog/code-execution-competitions)). For this challenge: `submission.zip` with `main.py` at root is unzipped into `/code_execution/`; data at `/code_execution/data/submission_format.csv` and `data/niftis/<uid>.nii.gz`; Python 3.12 with locked `uv.lock`; no package installs at inference; GPU image needs ≥21 GB disk; **smoke tests run on a subset of the *training* set** and are not scored ([runtime repo](https://github.com/drivendataorg/competition-sfmn-parkinsons-runtime), [data-access thread](https://community.drivendata.org/t/accessing-data-in-code-execution-environment/11467)). Test-set size and public/private split are **not disclosed**; the **best private-LB submission counts automatically** — no final selection ([thread](https://community.drivendata.org/t/test-set-size-and-final-submission-selection/11493)). Winners must release under MIT; external weights must be public and commercially licensed; no test-time pseudo-labelling; each scan processed independently ([rules](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/311/dat-parkinsons-challenge/rules/)). Upload time-outs were a reported failure mode ([thread](https://community.drivendata.org/t/problem-with-submissions/11497)). The 1×A100 / 3 h limit is from the brief (page not fetchable) **[unverified here]**.

**Winner engineering patterns.**
- **Overlap CPU preprocessing with GPU inference:** TissueNet 1st "costly operations that can be deferred to the CPU run in the background while the GPU is busy" ([blog](https://drivendata.co/blog/tissuenet-cervical-biopsies-winners/)). For us: registration (1.6–1.8 s/scan) in a process pool while the GPU consumes a queue.
- **Mixed-precision inference** for speed/memory (Kelp 2nd, [blog](https://drivendata.co/blog/kelp-wanted-winners)).
- **Big ensembles are normal and fit:** Kelp 2nd = 10-fold ensemble; Whale-do 1st = 12 models, 4th = 15 (3 × 5-fold); BioMassters 3rd = 15 models; but BioMassters **1st was a single model** ([blogs](https://drivendata.co/blog/biomass-winners)). Our run-2 zip (15 checkpoints, 50.7 MB, ~12 min extrapolated for 2 k scans) has ≥10× headroom for more checkpoints.
- **Cut inference-time search:** OSIC 1st dropped quantile selection to reach 3 min ([repo](https://github.com/artkulak/osic-pulmonary-fibrosis-progression)).
- **Fail-safes (DrivenData pattern + our run 2):** per-scan `try/except` cascade (full ensemble → 2.5D-only → ROI-LR → prior 0.548), wall-clock budget check that degrades to fewer checkpoints when behind schedule, deterministic output ordering from `submission_format.csv`, assert row count and [0,1] range before writing.
- **Decision-theoretic post-processing only when the metric asks for it:** TissueNet 1st maximised expected reward under the cost matrix; for log loss the analogue is temperature + clipping, nothing else.
- **Submit diverse variants:** because the best private score counts, each of the 3 weekly slots should be a *different* blend (e.g. with/without 2.5D arm, with/without hedging), not a re-upload — ISIC's ≈0 CV/LB correlation for single models is the reason not to pick by public score ([arXiv 2010.05351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05351)).

## 7. Prioritised "try next" list for our pipeline

Arms: **A** 3D GroupNorm ResNet (registered 2 mm box), **B** 2.5D EfficientNet-B0/V2-S on 3 × 8 mm axial slabs, **C** registered-ROI logistic regression; combiner = weighted logits + temperature floored at 1. Costs assume Kaggle T4 (run-2 3D checkpoint ≈ 11 min; 2.5D EfficientNet-B0 on 3-slab inputs ≈ 5–8 min; Hamburg 194 k-param 2D ResNet ≈ 5–10 min).

| Rank | Change | Why (evidence) | Expected public log-loss gain | T4 cost | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Add arm D: Hamburg-style 12 mm slab (2 mm, 72×72 crop of the striatal plane after registration + 75th-percentile scaling) → tiny ResNet (1/1/2 blocks, 16/32/64), 5 folds × 3 seeds, mean of probabilities** | exact published recipe at 98 %/94 %/98 % on three sites ([PMC10957699](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10957699/)); shallow > deep at our n ([PAC 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8374100/)) | −0.02 to −0.05 (AUROC +0.01–0.02) | 2–3 GPU-h for 15 models | low–medium: depends on registration QC (<3 % fallback gate) |
| 2 | **Blur + noise ("unrealistic") augmentation on A, B, D**, incl. count-scaling to simulate ultra-low-count scans | +0.02–0.03 acc across sites ([JNM 2024](https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.267570)); Wenzel mixed-resolution robustness | −0.01 to −0.03 | retrain cost only (A: ~3 h for 15 ckpts; B: ~2 h; D: ~2.5 h) | low |

| 5 | **Clip final p to [0.02, 0.98]** | Kaggle log-loss practice ([fast.ai](https://forums.fast.ai/t/kaggle-evaluation-log-loss/235)) | 0 to −0.005 (tail insurance) | none | none |
| 6 | **Auxiliary head on A/B/D predicting registered-ROI SBRs (putamen/caudate L/R, asymmetry)** | RSNA23 aux loss +0.01–0.03 ([repo](https://github.com/Nischaydnk/RSNA-2023-1st-place-solution)); ISIC richer target | −0.005 to −0.01 | ~0 extra per run | low |

**Suggested order for the remaining ~46 GPU-h:** (2)+(3) as one retrain of A and B with blur/noise and 5 seeds (~10 h) → (1) arm D (~3 h) → (6) folded into the same runs if the aux head is ready → LOCO refit of the combiner with (4)+(5) → submission #1 of the week; then (7)/(8)/(10) as diversity variants for submissions #2–3. Expected: 0.339 → ~0.25 from run 3 alone (brief), and the items above are the evidence-backed path from 0.25 toward the 0.22–0.24 top-10 band, since each +0.01 AUROC ≈ −0.03 log loss on the binormal curve.
