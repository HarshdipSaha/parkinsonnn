# Literature Review — DaT-SPECT / DaTscan Classification for Parkinson's Disease

Research date: 2026-09-05
Purpose: survey prior academic work and datasets on automated DaT-SPECT interpretation, to inform the modeling approach for the [DaT Parkinson's Challenge](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/311/dat-parkinsons-challenge/) (see `DAT_PARKINSONS_CHALLENGE.md` in this repo for competition rules/logistics).

## TL;DR — what this means for "the idea"

1. **This is a well-trodden problem.** Multiple independent groups have hit **96–99% accuracy / AUC 0.98–0.997** on binary normal-vs-abnormal DaT-SPECT classification since 2017. The core signal (striatal dopamine transporter uptake asymmetry/intensity) is strong and learnable — this is *not* a case where deep learning struggles to find signal.
2. **Almost all of that prior work leans on one dataset: PPMI.** And **PPMI is explicitly disallowed for prize eligibility** in this competition (DrivenData staff confirmed this in the forum — see below). So the field's dominant pretraining/transfer-learning source is off-limits if you want to compete for the €25k. This is the single biggest strategic constraint on "the idea."
3. **The competition's actual hard part is different from what most papers solved.** Most published results are single-center or two-center, with images already spatially normalized to a common template (MNI space, PPMI's standard pipeline). This competition's data is **10 hospitals, native/varying voxel spacing, no guarantee of pre-registration** — i.e., the domain-generalization problem the literature mostly doesn't have to deal with. That's where a competitive edge is likely to come from, not from a bigger/fancier network.
4. **Reported near-100% numbers should be discounted.** Several papers (including one of the most-cited, Quan et al. 2019) explicitly admit their own numbers are "likely optimistically swayed" by small test sets, slice-selection shortcuts, and PPMI's relatively curated/homogeneous acquisition. Only Choi et al. 2017 and Zhao et al. 2022 validate on a genuinely independent cohort/center, and their numbers hold up well there too — that's the more trustworthy signal about what's achievable.
5. **Classical radiomics + LASSO + simple classifiers (SVM/KNN/LDA) matches deep learning** (AUC ~0.99) in at least one large PPMI study. With a "very small" training set (per a competition forum participant) and log-loss scoring (which punishes overconfidence), a lean pipeline — good preprocessing/registration, a handful of interpretable features or a modest 3D CNN, strong calibration — is a very defensible strategy versus chasing a large from-scratch deep 3D architecture.

## 1. Prior work (chronological)

| Year | Study | Data | Method | Headline result | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2017 | **Choi et al.**, *Refining diagnosis of PD with deep learning-based interpretation of DAT imaging* ([PMC5610036](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5610036)) | PPMI train/val (549: 379 PD/170 NC), PPMI test (75), **independent** SNUH cohort (82: 72 PD/10 non-parkinsonian tremor), 77 SWEDD cases | 3D CNN ("PD Net") on MNI-normalized, attenuation-corrected volumes (91×109×91, 2mm³) | PPMI test: **96.0% acc, AUC 0.988**. Independent SNUH cohort: **98.8% acc, AUC 0.997** | The field's seminal/most-cited paper. Notably validated on a *genuinely separate* hospital/cohort and still held up — the single strongest generalization evidence in this literature. Also found 80% of ambiguous "SWEDD" cases flipped to abnormal on 2-year follow-up, i.e. real diagnostic value in exactly the ambiguous cases this competition targets. |
| 2019 | **Quan et al.**, *DaTscan SPECT Image Classification for PD* ([arXiv:1909.04142](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04142)) | PPMI, 659 unique subjects (449 PD / 210 NC) | Transfer learning (InceptionV3) on 3 concatenated axial slices (MNI space) | **98.5% acc, AUC 0.991** | Authors themselves caveat: small dataset, only 3 slices used (not full volume), results "likely optimistically swayed." Good cautionary example of what to *not* over-trust. |
| 2021 | **Nazari et al.**, *Explainable AI to improve acceptance of CNNs for DAT SPECT classification* (EJNMMI, [PMC8921148](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8921148/)) | PPMI | CNN + explainability (saliency/Grad-CAM-style) | High accuracy (exact figure not independently re-extracted here; PMC page blocked by reCAPTCHA on fetch) | Explicitly targets the "black box" trust problem for clinicians — relevant since this competition's models are meant for real clinical deployment (BOAS registry), not just leaderboard score. |
| 2022 | **An Ensemble of CNN Models for PD Detection Using DaTscan Images** ([PMC9139649](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9139649/)) | PPMI, 645 images (432 PD/213 NC) | Ensemble of VGG16 + ResNet50 + InceptionV3 + Xception via "Fuzzy Rank Level Fusion," single central slice (91×109×91 → slice 41, 224×224 PNG) | **98.45% acc, F1 98.84%** | Ensembling individually-decent (93–95% acc) 2D transfer-learning models beat every single model and simpler fusion rules (sum/product/majority vote). Single-slice input again — a recurring shortcut in this literature. |
| 2022 | **Zhao et al.**, *Decoding DAT imaging for differential diagnosis of parkinsonism using deep learning* (EJNMMI, [PMC9206631](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9206631/)) | **1,017 subjects**, single center (Huashan, Shanghai), [11C]CFT **PET** (not SPECT, but same DAT-imaging concept) | 3D residual CNN ("DAT-Net") with skip connections, full-gradient saliency maps | CV: AUC 0.90–0.95 across IPD/MSA/PSP. **Blind test**: IPD sens/spec 90.7%/88.4%, MSA 84.1%/97.5%, PSP 78.6%/93.3%. Beat conventional binding-ratio analysis (p<0.0001) | Largest cohort in this table; crucially reports a genuine **blind held-out test**, and performance drops noticeably vs. CV (as expected) — a realistic reference point for what a rigorous held-out score looks like versus PPMI-only CV numbers. Saliency maps correctly highlighted putamen/caudate/midbrain — consistent with known neuroanatomy. |
| 2022 | Radiomics signature paper ([PMC9237203](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9237203/)) | PPMI, 413 subjects (312 PD/101 NC), split by **SPECT manufacturer** into train + 2 separate test sets | 930 radiomics features (GLCM/GLRLM/GLSZM/GLDZM/NGTDM/NGLDM across caudate/putamen/pallidum) → LASSO → 5 features → SVM/KNN/LDA/decision tree | **AUC 0.990 / 0.986** across the two manufacturer-stratified test sets; sens/spec 98.6%/88.0% and 100%/85.0% | Important: this is **classical ML, no deep learning**, and it explicitly tests **cross-scanner generalization** by manufacturer — directly relevant to this competition's multi-center/multi-scanner setup. Shows a lean, interpretable radiomics pipeline is competitive with CNNs. |
| 2026 (Apr) | **Revisiting CNN-Based PD Classification from DaT-SPECT Images: The Role of Training Protocols** ([MDPI Electronics 15(9):1883](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/15/9/1883)) | Two PPMI-derived DaT-SPECT datasets; faithfully reproduces a prior published setup on a curated **645-image** dataset | Systematic ablation of training *protocol* (data augmentation, transfer learning, weight decay, LR scheduling) holding architecture fixed | Not independently re-extracted in full (site blocked direct fetch; summary from indexed abstract) | **Most directly relevant paper to this challenge's actual bottleneck**: its whole thesis is that *architecture choice matters less than training-protocol discipline* for small/moderate DaT-SPECT datasets — exactly the situation here (small training set per forum, multi-center variability). Worth getting full text before finalizing a training pipeline. |

**Also found but not deeply reviewed** (broader-scope, lower direct relevance):
- *A Computerized Analysis with ML Techniques for the Diagnosis of PD: Past Studies and Future Perspectives* ([PMC9689408](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9689408/)) — 217-paper survey across **all** PD data modalities (speech, gait, handwriting, EEG, wearables, neuroimaging). DaTSCAN/SPECT accuracy range cited: **95–99%**. Cross-cutting pitfalls it flags: small/imbalanced samples, non-standardized evaluation protocols, weak clinical validation, reproducibility gaps — all apply directly here.
- *A Methodological and Structural Review of PD Detection Across Diverse Data Modalities* (2025, [arXiv:2505.00525](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.00525)) — broad multi-modality review; DaT-SPECT coverage is thin, mainly namechecks PPMI/NTUA and cites Choi et al. 2017-adjacent work (98% accuracy) without deep analysis.
- Ebrahimian Sadabad et al. 2026, *Detection of PD with neuroimaging modalities using ML and AI: a systematic review* (Neurological Sciences, [doi:10.1007/s10072-025-08768-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10072-025-08768-6)) — listed on the competition's own "Learn more" reading list; full text is paywalled (Springer login-gated), only bibliographic record confirmed.
- Booij et al. 1998 ([PMID 9829575](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9829575/)) — foundational nuclear-medicine paper on FP-CIT SPECT imaging itself (not ML), also cited by the competition's own "About" page as clinical background.

### Common methodological pattern across almost all of the above
1. Spatially normalize/register each scan to a standard template (usually **MNI space**, via SPM or similar), producing a fixed-size volume (~91×109×91, 2mm³) — this is PPMI's standard pipeline and is why most published methods can assume a fixed input shape.
2. Either (a) select 1–3 "best" axial slices through the striatum and treat it as a 2D/2.5D image (feeding ImageNet-pretrained 2D CNNs — InceptionV3, VGG16, ResNet50, Xception — via transfer learning), or (b) run a full 3D CNN over the whole normalized volume.
3. Small-to-moderate sample sizes throughout (400–1,000 subjects is typical; Zhao et al.'s 1,017 is the largest found).
4. Reported metrics are almost always accuracy/AUC/sensitivity/specificity — **none of the papers surveyed optimize for log loss / calibration**, which is what this competition actually scores on.

## 3. Synthesis: what this means for the modeling approach

**What's proven to work (from the literature):**
- Registration/spatial-normalization to a common template before modeling is close to universal practice, and directly addresses this competition's variable-spacing/variable-dimension problem — this should be a first-class preprocessing step, not an afterthought.
- Both full-3D CNNs and 2D/2.5D slice-based transfer learning have reached similar top-line performance in the literature; a radiomics + classical-ML pipeline matched them and explicitly handled cross-scanner generalization. Given a small training set, the radiomics/lean-model route (or a small 3D CNN with heavy transfer/augmentation per the 2026 training-protocols paper) is lower-risk than a large from-scratch deep network.
- Saliency/explainability (Zhao et al., Nazari et al.) is cheap to add and aligns with the sponsor's stated goal of clinician trust/adoption (BOAS registry deployment) — could be a differentiator in the post-challenge collaboration angle even if it doesn't move the log-loss leaderboard.

**What's genuinely different/harder here than in the literature:**
- No confirmed prize-eligible pretraining corpus (PPMI ruled out, NTUA doubtful) — training is realistically from-scratch on the competition's own small, multi-center set, which argues for parameter-efficient models, strong regularization/augmentation, and cross-validation over anything resembling the deep transfer-learning stacks used in most cited papers.
- True multi-center domain shift (10 hospitals, native varying voxel spacing) is more severe than most published PPMI-based studies, which are pre-harmonized. The radiomics paper's manufacturer-stratified test and Zhao et al.'s single-center blind test are the closest literature analogues to "does this generalize," and both show a measurable (if modest) drop from CV to true held-out performance — a useful expectation-setting data point (don't expect leaderboard log loss to match CV loss).
- The metric is **log loss**, which none of the cited papers optimize for — calibration (temperature scaling / Platt / isotonic on a held-out fold) is likely to matter more for this competition's ranking than squeezing out extra raw accuracy.

**Suggested next step before committing to an architecture:** get the full text of the 2026 MDPI training-protocols paper (currently only abstract-level here) — its explicit focus on protocol-over-architecture for small DaT-SPECT datasets is the single most directly applicable piece of prior work found.
